# MATHMON — World Lore, Storyline & Narrative Bible

This is the canon the game's story data is written from (`data/story/*.json`). If the two ever
disagree, fix the data. All story is shown as on-screen text — there is no narration audio.

---

## 1. Mathea and the Numera

**Before the beginning there was the Null** — not emptiness, but something *undefined*: nothing
could be counted, compared or measured, so nothing could be said to exist.

Then **five Axioms** woke, five truths so simple they could not be false:

| Axiom | Statement | Root it became | Region above it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The First | *There is one.* | shattered into light | Crystal Realm (the Prism Archive) |
| Counting | *One can follow one.* | the Root of Counting | Number Forest |
| Balance | *Equal things stay equal.* | the Root of Balance | Algebra Cavern |
| Form | *Between any two points, a line.* | the Root of Form | Geometry Kingdom |
| Change | *What changes, changes by a rule.* | the Root of Change | Chronos Dimension |

Wherever an Axiom *held*, reality became countable, comparable and lasting. That holding force
is the **Numera** — Mathematical Energy. It runs through the five Axiom Roots under Mathea, and
the Roots cross at one place: the **Zero Point**, where all of mathematics starts counting.

This is why every region feels different: each sits on a different Root. Forest trees grow in
counting spirals; the Cavern's crystals form in balanced pairs; the Kingdom's stones fracture at
exact angles; and in Chronos, time itself ticks in sequences.

## 2. The Firstborn — where MathMon come from

Where Numera pooled, it began to **repeat itself**. A repeating pattern that learned to keep its
own shape became alive: the first **MathMon**. They are not animals. A MathMon's body is a
pattern holding itself together; animal shapes are how young Numera imitates the life around it.
Older, stronger MathMon stop imitating and become **stranger** — geometric beings, living symbols,
creatures of light — because their pattern no longer needs a borrowed shape.

**Why maths powers battles.** A MathMon's pattern is steadied by *understanding*. When a trainer
truly works something out, the bond carries that clarity into their partner and its move lands
true. Guessing makes the pattern wobble. (This is the in-world reason every move asks a question.)

## 3. The four civilisations

1. **The Tallyfolk** (Number Forest) — the first people to befriend MathMon, by *counting with
   them*. They raised counting stones, the **Arch of Sums** and the first Numera Seals.
2. **The Order of the Unknown** (Algebra Cavern) — scholars who learned that Numera could be
   *stored* in an unknown: an x is a promise that a value exists. Their laboratories bottled it.
3. **Euclidia, the Kingdom of Form** (Geometry Kingdom) — architects who shaped Numera into
   buildings that could not fall, as long as every angle was exact.
4. **The Timekeepers** (Chronos Dimension) — measurers of the Root of Change, keepers of the
   **Great Clock** that sequences Mathea's days.

## 4. The Last Calculation and the Unravelling (about 1,000 years ago)

The four civilisations united to build the **Origin Engine** at the Zero Point. Its purpose: the
**Last Calculation** — to compute the *Final Number*, the exact total of all Numera. With it,
nothing in Mathea would ever be uncertain, lost or wrong again.

The calculation did not come out even. It left a **Remainder** — Numera that fits no pattern.
Remainder makes patterns stutter: MathMon turn wild, structures wake, numbers drift in the air.
This was **the Unravelling**. The civilisations sealed the Remainder behind **Numera Seals** in
every region and set **guardians** over the seals — and then they faded. Their last records all
say the same thing in different words: *never complete the calculation.*

**The truth the ancients only half-understood:** a remainder is not an error. In division, what
is left over is where the *next* count begins. The Remainder is the part of the Numera that lets
anything **new** happen — change, choice, learning. A world with no remainder would be perfect,
and perfectly frozen.

## 5. The present-day mystery (revealed in layers)

| Layer | What the player believes | What is revealed | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Numera is fading by natural decay (the Professor says so). | — | Prologue |
| 2 | Restore the seals and it will stop. | The seals were **broken recently** — fresh tool marks, new ink. The corruption is a *Remainder* and is ancient. | Ch 1 |
| 3 | Someone is breaking seals. | A hooded stranger is **feeding Remainder into the guardians** to collect it. Dr. Vexa's records mention two researchers, "N." and "V.", who reopened the Order's lab 20 years ago. | Ch 2 |
| 4 | The Professor is a trustworthy guide. | Euclidian records say the Unravelling was **caused by the calculation**, not decay — and a lab log fragment in the Professor's own hand: *"I told the council it was natural decay."* | Ch 3 |
| 5 | The stranger is the villain. | The stranger is **Vera Tallow**, the Professor's former partner. Twenty years ago they restarted the Origin Engine together. The Professor skipped checking one step; the Engine misfired, and Vera's home village in Chronos was frozen in time with her family inside. He hid it. | Ch 4 |
| 6 | Stop Vera, save the world. | Vera wants to finish the Last Calculation *correctly* so that nothing can ever go wrong again — but a world without a remainder is frozen like her village. The answer is not to complete the calculation, but to understand the Remainder. | Final |

## 6. Characters

**Professor Archibald Numeris** — *primary narrator.* Brilliant, curious, funny, forgetful (his
glasses are usually on his head), protective, obsessed with ancient civilisations. His motto,
*"Mistakes are just data!"*, sounds like a joke until you learn it is a vow: he once hid a mistake,
and it cost Vera everything. He gave up Engine research to teach young trainers to *check their
work*. He knows more than he says and deflects with jokes or sudden interest in the weather.
Clues: he flinches at the word "Remainder"; he keeps a drawer locked; his lab notes jump from
"Year 0" to "Year 20"; he never visits Chronos.
*Arc:* evasive guide → exposed → confesses (how depends on your choices) → works with Vera to
settle the Remainder.

**Vera Tallow** — *antagonist.* A Timekeeper descendant and once the Professor's partner; precise,
quiet, relentless, grieving. She wears a pocket watch stopped at 3:14 — the moment her village
froze. She is right that the Professor lied and right that mistakes hurt people. She is wrong
that removing every remainder will save anyone. First appears as **the Hooded Stranger**.

**Rex** — the rival. Loud, fast, secretly hard-working; grows from show-off to friend and admits
he practises his times tables every night.

**Sage Ilo** (Sanctuary keeper, Tallyfolk descendant) — remembers the old songs about the Seals.
**Old Abacus** (village historian) — eccentric, collects inscriptions, gets dates slightly wrong
on purpose to see if you notice. **Mira**, **Bram**, **Tamsin**, **Penny**, **Nurse Hana**,
**Tobi** (a child who lost his DIVOWL) — village life. **Dr. Vexa** (Algebra Cavern) — once
assisted "N. and V."; nervous, honest. **Sir Hypot** (Geometry Kingdom) — knight of Euclidia's
last line, formal and kind. **Keeper Tick** (Chronos) — the last Timekeeper still moving in time;
he knew Vera as a child.

## 7. Chapters

| Chapter | Region | Maths | Main quest | Boss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue — A World of Numbers | Starter Village | counting | (meet the Professor) | — |
| 1 — The Forgotten Numbers | Number Forest | + − × ÷, patterns, fractions | THE FADING NUMERA | THE ARITHMETIC BEAST |
| 2 — The Fractured Equations | Algebra Cavern | unknowns, equations | THE UNKNOWN GUARDIAN | THE UNKNOWN GUARDIAN |
| 3 — The Broken Dimensions | Geometry Kingdom | shape, angle, area, volume | THE DIMENSION DRAGON | THE DIMENSION DRAGON |
| 4 — The Origin of Chaos | Chronos Dimension | sequences, time, advanced algebra | THE INFINITE OVERLORD | THE INFINITE OVERLORD |
| Final — The Last Calculation | the Zero Point (Time Spire summit) | everything | THE LAST CALCULATION | Vera's calculation |

**Prologue.** The Professor welcomes the trainer, explains the Numera and MathMon, gives them the
**Num-Link** (a text communicator) and their partner. The village Counting Stone flickers and its
numbers fall off; far away, the old arch in the ruins pulses purple. *First mission: find out why.*

**Chapter 1.** Green Meadow MathMon behave oddly; Tallyfolk counting stones and the Arch's
inscription reveal the word *Remainder*. The seals in the Ancient Number Ruins have fresh
scratches. After the Arithmetic Beast falls, a **hooded stranger** watches from the arch and
leaves — and a cave opens beneath the ruins.

**Chapter 2.** The Order's laboratory. Dr. Vexa's notes mention "N. and V." The guardian EQUINOX
was *injected* with Remainder. **Revelation:** the stranger is collecting Remainder from every
guardian on purpose.

**Chapter 3.** Euclidia's ruined halls contradict the Professor: the Unravelling was man-made.
A burnt lab log in the Professor's handwriting is found in the Dragon Keep. **Choice:** confront
the Professor over the Num-Link, or keep it to yourself for now.

**Chapter 4.** Time stands still. Keeper Tick tells you about Vera's frozen village. The
**Professor's journal** (secret quest) and the stranger's unmasking reveal the whole truth. After
the Infinite Overlord, Vera opens the way to the Zero Point.

**Final.** At the Origin Engine, Vera begins the Last Calculation. You must answer alongside it —
a question from every chapter. Then you choose.

### Endings
* **The Sealed Engine** — you seal the Engine forever. The Remainder sleeps; Vera leaves alone.
  The Professor's secret stays buried. Mathea is safe — and a little sadder.
* **The Open Remainder** — you persuade Vera to stop. The Professor confesses. The Engine is shut
  down; time in Chronos slowly begins to tick again.
* **The Remainder Theorem** *(true ending — needs the Professor's journal and at least 12 historical
  records)* — you show Vera the ancients' last record: the remainder is where the next count begins.
  She and the Professor finish the work *together* — not the calculation, but the understanding.
  Her village wakes. The Professor's last line: *"Mistakes are just data. It took me twenty years
  to learn what that means."*

## 8. Regions (environmental identity)

* **Starter Village** — whitewashed houses, the Counting Stone in the square, the Mathmart, the
  Monster Sanctuary, the Recovery Center, the Professor's lab.
* **Green Meadow** — counting-spiral grass and Tallyfolk tally stones.
* **Ancient Number Ruins** — fallen counting arches; the Seals of Sums, Products and Parts.
* **Pine Ridge, Waterfall Plateau, Sunsand Desert, Mirror Beach, the farms and pasture** — the
  wider forest; the Crystal Seal, the rune tablet, Bram's bridge.
* **Algebra Cavern** — Cave Mouth camp, Glow Tunnels, Echo Hollow, the Lost Laboratory. Crystals
  grow in balanced pairs; unsolved equations glow on the walls.
* **Geometry Kingdom** — Temple Gate, Garden Court, Pillar Court, the Dragon Keep. Obelisks cast
  shadows at exact angles; paths are perfectly straight.
* **Chronos Dimension** — Clock Gate, Tick and Tock Isles, the Time Spire. Islands float in frozen
  time; clock towers all show 3:14.
* **Crystal Realm (the Prism Archive)** — where the First Axiom shattered into light; optional.
* **The Zero Point** — beneath the Time Spire, where the five Roots cross: the Origin Engine.

## 9. MathMon encyclopedia

Every species has a codex entry in `data/story/bestiary.json` (concept, habitat, behaviour,
relationships, history, story role). Low-level MathMon are Numera imitating animals; the Trial
monsters and the bosses are older and more abstract. Future species should lean into abstract
entities, living symbols, ancient constructs and celestial beings rather than more animals.
